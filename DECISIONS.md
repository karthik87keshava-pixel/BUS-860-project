## Vehicle data

- **2026-10-28** — Source: NRCan Fuel Consumption Ratings, all six English tables (conventional 1995–2014, 2015–2024, 2025, 2026; battery-electric 2012–2026; plug-in hybrid 2012–2026), downloaded through the open.canada.ca CKAN data API (`datastore_search`) because the direct file links redirect to a storage host that was blocked in our build environment. Same data either way.
- **2026-10-28** — Result: **17,569 vehicle versions, 2010–2026, 56 makes, 667 model families** (see `docs/data-coverage.md` for counts per year and fuel type).
- **2026-10-28** — Rows not used (820, listed in the coverage report): 815 E85 ratings of flex-fuel vehicles (their gasoline rating is kept, since Alberta drivers buy gasoline), 1 natural-gas vehicle (not sold to the public), 4 exact duplicates.
- **2026-10-28** — NRCan does not flag hybrids in the conventional tables. A vehicle is marked hybrid when its name says so (Hybrid, Prius, Lexus "h" models, etc.) or when the EPA lists the exact same model as a hybrid. 46 vehicles were changed this way and are listed in `docs/data-coverage.md`. Mild hybrids (MHEV, Buick eAssist) stay "gas". Model years sold only as hybrids in Canada (2026 RAV4, Camry 2025+, Sienna 2021+, Venza 2021+, Crown, Crown Signia, Sequoia 2023+, Land Cruiser 2024+) are marked hybrid for every version.
- **2026-10-28** — EPA (fueleconomy.gov) data is also used to tell small from large SUVs for 2010–2014 (NRCan uses one "SUV" class those years) and to fill drive type. All consumption figures stay NRCan's.
- **2026-10-28** — Plug-in hybrids: NRCan gives only a combined electric rating, used for both city and highway. Some PHEVs still burn a little gas on battery ("[20.0 kWh + 0.2 L]/100 km"); that gas is counted.
- **2026-10-28** — "Model family" names (RAV4, F-150, 3 Series…) are built from NRCan names with rules per make, so the Model picker is tidy. Each NRCan row is a "version" (name + engine + transmission + rating).
- **2026-10-28** — Vehicle data is stored as a JSON file inside the site and searched on the server, **not in the Supabase database** as the brief suggested. Reasons: search is instant without a database round trip, the site works even if Supabase is paused (free projects pause after 7 days idle), and a yearly refresh is just "run the script, push". Supabase stores only accounts and saved comparisons. Easy to move later if needed.
- **2026-10-28** — Each vehicle has a stable ID built from its NRCan details, so saved comparisons and share links keep working after a data refresh.

- **2026-11-09** — **List made exhaustive with EPA data (team request).** The team noticed only two 2026 Infinitis. That one is correct (Infiniti ended the Q50 after 2024 and the QX50/QX55 after 2025; the EPA also lists only the QX60 and QX80 for 2026), but the check showed real gaps: NRCan has not rated any 2027 models yet, although many are on sale this fall, and it skipped some models in some years. We now add the US EPA rating wherever the EPA rated a make + model family in a year NRCan has no row for: **1,309 versions** (753 for 2027, the rest filling gaps in 2010–2026), marked "Fuel rating from the US EPA" on the site and listed in docs/data-coverage.md. Rules: the family must appear in NRCan in some year (to keep US-only models out), except brand-new 2026–2027 nameplates (e.g. Infiniti QX65, Kia EV3, Audi Q9, Lexus TZ, Rivian R2, Volvo EX60), which carry a note to check Canadian availability. EPA names mapped to Canadian ones (Outlander Sport → RVR, "3" → Mazda3, GLI → Jetta, GTI → Golf GTI). McLaren and Saab (sold in Canada but missing from NRCan's tables) added. Hydrogen and natural-gas vehicles left out. New total: **18,878 versions, 2010–2027.**
- **2026-11-09** — In late 2026 both 2026 and 2027 models are sold new, so both default to "new"; older years default to "used" unless they are the newest year of that model.
- **2026-11-09** — Models with no base price yet get the median price of the same make and size class until a base price is added; all new nameplates found so far have their own estimate.

## Prices

- **2026-10-28** — No free, complete Canadian MSRP database exists (the federal iZEV data has sales counts, not prices). Method, in order:
  1. A base Canadian MSRP for every model family (`data/prices/family-prices.csv`, 668 families) — our estimate from typical Canadian launch prices, marked **estimate**.
  2. Published 2026 base prices found in free public sources (Toyota Canada and Mazda Canada press releases, Auto123, CarCostCanada, Autotrader.ca research pages, Drive Tesla Canada, ThinkEV) in `data/prices/verified-prices.csv`, with link and date, marked **verified** — 35 rows covering 31 best-selling models. Where sources disagreed or only gave US prices (Ram 1500, Silverado, Escape, Maverick, Sorento, Grand Cherokee, Tiguan, Jetta), we kept the estimate.
  3. Version adjustments (AWD +7%, bigger engine +12% per two cylinders, trim keywords +10% / +22% / +50% / +90%, fuel-type premiums when no fuel-specific price exists; cap 2.5×). A price is "verified" only when the shown number is exactly the published base price.
- **2026-10-28** — Result: every one of the 17,569 versions has a pre-filled price; 47 are verified exact base prices. Expect estimates to be off by 10–20% for a given trim. Spot check: 2026 Model Y Long Range AWD estimated at $53,500 vs about $60,000 actual.
- **2026-10-28** — Older model years are moved to their own year with Statistics Canada's CPI for new passenger vehicles (table 18-10-0005-01). **Not loaded yet**: www150.statcan.gc.ca is blocked in our build environment, so older years currently use the newer base price. The used-price step (below) shrinks the effect.
- **2026-10-28** — **Older model years are priced as used cars.** A 2015 car can only be bought used, so pre-filling its 2015 sticker price would mislead. Default condition is "new" only for the newest model year of each family (2025 or later); older years get "used", with price = original MSRP × share of value kept at its age. Users can switch new/used. Leases are always new.
- **2026-10-28** — Paid options (not used, per team decision): Canadian Black Book licenses data only to qualified companies through its sales team, with no public price list; used-car values are also available from VMR. Licensed car photo services (IMAGIN.studio, EVOX/FuelAPI, used by JATO) also price by quote. Both would need a sales conversation before a public launch.

- **2026-11-09** — Statistics Canada price index loaded (www150.statcan.gc.ca allowed). The annual table 18-10-0005-01 has "Purchase of passenger vehicles" (new and used together), not a new-only series, so that is what we use. Index 2010 = 90.8, 2015 = 95.8, 2020 = 108.4, 2025 = 129.5 (2002 = 100). Spot checks of resulting MSRPs: 2015 Civic $20,700 (actual ≈ $20,000), 2019 RAV4 $27,000 (≈ $28,000), 2012 Camry $23,500 (≈ $24,000).

- **2026-11-09** — 11 more verified 2026 prices added (Ram 1500, Pilot, Accord gas and hybrid, Sienna, Highlander, Palisade, Outback, Escape, Compass, Wrangler). Skipped because sources disagree: Maverick, Equinox EV. Kia skipped the 2026 Telluride model year, so its 2025 price stays an estimate.

- **2026-11-09** — 80 more verified 2026 prices: EVs (EV9, IONIQ 9, Mach-E, LEAF, Ariya, bZ, Solterra, Blazer EV, OPTIQ, EX30, EX40, Polestar 4, R1T, R1S, Cayenne Electric) and luxury models (BMW X3, X5, 3 Series; Mercedes GLC gas and plug-in, GLE; Audi Q5, A5; Lexus RX, NX, ES; Acura RDX, MDX; Volvo XC40, XC60 gas and plug-in, XC90; Genesis GV70, GV80; Porsche Macan). **141 versions verified in total.** Skipped because sources disagree or are out of date: Niro EV, Kona Electric, IONIQ 6, ID.4, Equinox EV, Audi Q7, Mercedes C-Class, Porsche 911. Kia EV6 has no 2026 model.
- **2026-11-09** — Rule fixes found while checking: fuel-type premiums are now also capped in dollars (hybrid $5,000, plug-in $12,000, electric $15,000), because a battery costs about the same on any car (2026 X5 plug-in: $120,300 → $104,500); BMW X-model M versions count as high-performance (X5 M Competition: $103,600 → $155,400); Cybertruck estimate raised to its $137,990 Canadian launch price (was $115,000).

## Alberta defaults (all editable; sources on "How we calculate")

- **2026-10-28** — Regular gas $1.55/L (Edmonton/Calgary Sept 2026 averages 151–156¢; Alberta suspended its 13¢ fuel tax on Oct 1, 2026). Premium $1.80, diesel $1.65 (estimates). Fuel +2%/year.
- **2026-10-28** — Home electricity $0.23/kWh all-in (Alberta average incl. delivery and transmission ~22.9¢). Public fast charging $0.60/kWh (Electrify Canada Alberta). 85% home charging when home charging = yes. NRCan kWh ratings include charging losses, so none are added.
- **2026-10-28** — Registration $93/year; Alberta EV tax $200/year for battery-electric only (since Feb 13, 2025; hybrids and PHEVs exempt).
- **2026-10-28** — Insurance: Alberta average $1,903 (AIRB 2026 report, 2025 data), treated as the premium for a car worth about $25,000, scaled by (value ÷ 25,000)^0.35 and by class (sports cars ×1.25). Clearly labelled as a rough estimate. +3%/year.
- **2026-10-28** — Winter: +15% fuel for gas cars (NRCan/EPA: 12–28% in city driving), +20% hybrids (estimate), +35% energy for EVs (CAA test 14–39% range loss; Recurrent ~30%), applied for the winter months only.
- **2026-10-28** — **Rebates checked:** the federal iZEV program is closed. The federal **Electric Vehicle Affordability Program (EVAP)** is active in 2026: up to $5,000 (EV) / $2,500 (PHEV), final transaction price $50,000 or less (no cap for Canadian-made), made in Canada or a free-trade partner country, one per person, applied at the dealer; drops to $4,000 on Jan 1, 2027. Leases of 48+ months get the full amount; 75/50/25% for 36/24/12 months (from dealer guides, not yet confirmed on the Transport Canada page). Default: applied automatically to new EVs/PHEVs under the cap; we can't check country of manufacture per vehicle, so the tooltip asks users to confirm. **Alberta has no provincial EV rebate.**
- **2026-10-28** — Federal luxury tax on new cars over $100,000 (lesser of 10% of price or 20% of the excess) is added automatically.
- **2026-10-28** — Dealer costs: freight/PDI $2,200 (new), documentation fee $599, A/C excise tax + tire levy $116 (new). GST 5% on price + fees − trade-in.
- **2026-10-28** — Loans: 6.6% APR new (Bank of Canada, spring 2026), 10.3% used, 72 months, 10% down. Leases: 4.99%, term = ownership period (24–60 months), 20,000 km/year, 12¢/km over, $650 acquisition, $400 disposition.
- **2026-10-28** — Maintenance per km: gas 7¢, diesel 8¢, hybrid 6.5¢, PHEV 6¢, EV 4¢ (ThinkEV 2026 annual ranges), ×1.05–1.2 for bigger vehicles, ×1.3 for luxury brands. Tires: winter set on rims at the start ($1,300–$2,200 by class), replacement sets every 60,000 km. Repairs: $600/year after a 3-year warranty ($450 EV, ×1.5 luxury). Home Level 2 charger $2,000 installed.
- **2026-10-28** — Depreciation: Canadian Black Book reports cars keep about 72–80% after 1 year, 52–61% after 3, 40–45% after 5, and the average 4-year-old keeps 57%. Our curve: 78% after year 1, then ×0.89 a year to year 5 (62% at 3, 49% at 5), then ×0.92 a year (older cars lose value more slowly). Strong-resale brands and pickups +4/+2 points, weaker European luxury brands −4/−3, EVs −6/−3 (estimates).
- **2026-10-28** — Default driving: 40 km a day, 7 days a week (14,560 km/year), 55% city, 5 years, 5 winter months.

## Calculation choices

- **2026-10-28** (approved with the plan) — Loan: dealer fees and GST are financed. Loan total = down payment + trade-in + payments in the period + balance still owing at the end; resale is subtracted in "Net cost".
- **2026-10-28** — Lease: Canadian formula (depreciation part + (cap cost + residual) × money factor, money factor = rate% ÷ 2400), GST on each payment and on the down payment. Lease shorter than the period → a similar new lease is assumed (with a note). Longer → only months inside the period, no early-exit penalty. Buyout at residual shown, never added.
- **2026-10-28** — Cash (team rule): purchase price excluded by default, together with resale; visible toggle "Include purchase price for cash"; note shown in the column.
- **2026-10-28** — "Lowest cost" badge: lowest net cost; if any cash column excludes its price, it compares running costs only, and the page says so.
- **2026-10-28** — Per month and per km use the net cost. The chart shows "cost if you stopped at the end of each year" (paid so far + loan balance − value of the car then), so every line ends at the column's net cost.
- **2026-10-28** — No discounting (time value of money). Listed as a limit on "How we calculate"; could be an option in December.
- **2026-10-28** — Units: miles mode shows miles, US mpg (235.2 ÷ L/100 km) and miles per kWh. All maths stays metric.

- **2026-11-09** — Vercel refuses to store the Supabase publishable key under a `NEXT_PUBLIC_` name (it treats it as a leaked secret). The key is public by design, so the site now reads `SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE` on the server and passes them to the browser through the root layout. The secret key is never used.

- **2026-11-09** — Sign-in links use Supabase's "implicit" flow instead of "PKCE". With PKCE the emailed link only works in the browser that asked for it; opening it in an email app's built-in browser sent users back to the sign-in page. The sign-in page now also explains expired or used links.

- **2026-11-09** — **Google sign-in dropped (team decision).** Google Cloud's sign-up pushed a 90-day free trial that bills afterwards; the team chose not to sign up. (Creating a Google login client is free and doesn't need the trial, so it can be added later in about 10 minutes.) Email sign-in meets the brief. The site shows the Google button only when Google is switched on in Supabase, so nothing visible is broken.
- **2026-11-09** — Supabase's built-in email service allows only a few sign-in emails per hour per project ("email rate limit exceeded" seen in testing). Free fix if needed for a class demo: send through the team Gmail (custom SMTP, app password) and raise the limit — steps in docs/SETUP.md.

- **2026-11-09** — **Renamed to Fairdrive** (tagline "The fair cost of every kilometre.") and a design pass, with no change to calculations, formulas, data sources or default values: a one-line plain-English summary above the results (based on net cost after resale, the same number as the "Lowest cost" badge; it names the cheapest car, the saving against the next cheapest, and, when another car is cheaper to run but dearer overall, the main reasons: price, interest or resale value); net cost after resale as the headline number with the gap to each other car; result sections collapsed by default showing a subtotal row (sums of existing results), with "Expand all"; input boxes visibly different from calculated values; teal brand accent (5.47:1 with white text, passes WCAG AA; darker teal 7.58:1 for hover); chart lines labelled at their end and the final-year gap marked; "Try an example" (2025 RAV4 Hybrid AWD vs 2025 Model Y Long Range, both on a loan, default settings) and a live example card on the home page; display-only clean-up of Tesla factory codes in names ("Long Range AWD-B" → "Long Range AWD"), repeated words in the spec line, and a non-clickable New/Used tag; photo matching now skips photos of a different version (e.g. a RAV4 Prime plug-in photo for a RAV4 Hybrid).

- **2026-11-12** — **Questionnaire and usability round (class feedback, Session 3).** The version presented in class is commit `ca2c871` (local tag `v1-session3`); to go back, see "Going back to the previous version" in README.md. No change to formulas, data sources or default values: all 20 tests pass unchanged. What changed:
  - **A short questionnaire replaces the "Your driving" panel at the top.** It opens once for a new visitor (never for share links or saved comparisons) and every question can be skipped. Each answer only fills an input that already existed: typical daily distance (under 20 km → 15, 20–50 → 35, 50–100 → 75, over 100 → 120 km a day, or the user's own yearly km); when they drive (mostly rush hour → 75% city, a mix → 55%, mostly off-peak or open highway → 30%), which answers the class comment that the Henday at 5 pm is city driving and at 7 pm is highway driving; years kept (1–8); where they live (Southern Alberta 4 winter months, Calgary area 5, Edmonton and central 5, Northern 6); and, only when an electric or plug-in car is in the comparison, home charging (yes → 85% at home, sometimes → 50%, no). **These mapped values are the team's estimates, not sourced figures.** The answers show as one line ("35 km a day · mostly rush hour · 5 years · Edmonton and central Alberta") with "Change answers"; all exact inputs moved to "Fine-tune the numbers" below the charts.
  - **Not done yet: driving style** (gentle / normal / sporty / aggressive) affecting fuel, tires and brakes. That changes the calculations, so it waits for sourced multipliers and team agreement. An extra charge for steady 110 km/h driving is also left out, because NRCan's ratings since 2015 already include some high-speed driving and we would risk counting it twice.
  - Results first: the one-sentence answer now sits directly under the page title; the "Show differences only" and "Include purchase price for cash" switches moved next to the table.
  - Phones: one or two cars now fit side by side without swiping (three or four still scroll); Share, Print and Save are one "Save or share" menu.
  - New / Used is a clear switch beside Loan / Lease / Cash (it was a small grey tag). The home example now uses new 2026 models (RAV4 AWD LE hybrid vs Model Y Long Range AWD), because 2025 models count as used in late 2026.
  - Plainer words: "Net cost after resale" is shown as "Your real cost over N years — after selling the car at the end" (the number is the same net cost). A switch shows the headline per month instead.
  - New "Where the money goes" bars: each car's net cost split into the car itself (paid, less resale), loan interest, fuel and electricity, insurance, maintenance and repairs, and tires/registration/other. Sums of existing results only; colours checked for colour-blind readers, with the values in a table underneath.
  - The winning column is tinted; each car shows "Verified price", "Estimated price", "Estimated used price" or "Your price" under its name; the home page starts with a search box ("2024 civic hybrid").
  - Checks: axe-core scan 0 issues on home, questionnaire and compare (desktop and 375 px); no sideways overflow; click-through of the questionnaire, example, monthly view and New/Used on both sizes.

- **2026-11-09** — Photo matching tightened during the design pass: a year in a Commons file name counts as the model year only when it is written right before the make ("2024 Toyota RAV4 …"), so photo dates ("… Sept 2025") are ignored; searches use exact phrases ("2025 Toyota RAV4", then one year either side, then the model); photos of another version (plug-in "Prime", "Electric", "4xe"…), motor-show cars and close-ups (brakes, mirrors…) are skipped. The 2025 RAV4 Hybrid had been shown a RAV4 Prime (plug-in) photo; it now shows a 2024 RAV4 Hybrid Woodland Edition photo (MercurySable99, CC BY-SA 4.0), stored in src/data/photos.json so it never depends on a live lookup. Wikimedia sometimes refuses busy servers (HTTP 429); the site retries once and caches successful lookups for 30 days.

## Design

- **2026-10-28** — Placeholder name used until the team chose one (alternatives offered: Kilo, TrueRide AB, CostLane). Accent deep teal `#0F766E` (5.5:1 contrast on white). Inter font. Layout modelled on the feel of a premium car-maker compare page (white space, black text, aligned rows, collapsible sections) without using any of its branding, fonts, photos or text.
- **2026-10-28** — The sticky column header shrinks to name + payment option once the photos scroll away, so it doesn't take a third of the screen. On phones, columns scroll sideways with snap and the header scrolls with them; the "Your driving" panel starts collapsed.
- **2026-10-28** — Share links hold the whole comparison in the URL (compressed), so they open for anyone without sign-in and need no database.
